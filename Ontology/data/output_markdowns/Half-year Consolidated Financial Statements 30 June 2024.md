1. BUSINESS ACTIVITIES

1.1. Presentation of the missions and business lines

A public group serving the financing and development of businesses, Bpifrance acts in support of the policies conducted
by the State and the Regions. The ordinance of 29 June 2005, amended by Law No. 2012-1559 of 31 December 2012
tasks it with promoting innovation, start-up, development, internationalisation, transfer and transmission of businesses,
by contributing to their financing through loans and equity.

Bpifrance is a credit institution and a holding company that acts through its subsidiaries, Bpifrance Régions (which
operates the Financing activities, together with the credit institution), Bpifrance Participations, Bpifrance Investissement
(an investment management company authorised and regulated by the AMF) and Bpifrance Assurance Export.

As a credit institution, Bpifrance is subject to banking regulations (Order of 3 November 2014 on the internal control of
companies in the banking, payment services and investment services sector subject to the supervision of the Prudential
Supervisory and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution)), which imposes numerous
obligations on it, such as operating under market conditions, without abusive support or ruinous credit, with a risk
prevention system (financial, image and operational), separation of roles, monitoring measurement systems and risk
control. Bpifrance and its subsidiary Bpifrance Régions are supervised by the Prudential Supervisory and Resolution
Authority and subject to direct prudential supervision by the European Central Bank1.

Bpifrance is a marketplace player, whose role is to bring together all financing partners for the benefit of companies,
banks first and foremost, as well as innovation development networks by co-financing with private banks.

Bpifrance provides financing to companies in their riskiest phases, such as business creation or transfer, innovation,
international development, as well as in the context of capacity investments, while respecting the rules of competition
law and private players. To do this, it relies on allocations from public actors at different levels: State, local authorities
(especially the Regions) and the European Union.

Its public interest mission requires Bpifrance to intervene as close as possible to the territories, in favour of VSEs, SMEs
and intermediate-sized enterprises, regardless of their legal status (including SSE companies2 and local public
companies3 performing a commercial activity), in all sectors of activity, except financial services.

Its public stature also implies a special welcome for companies in each Region, a search for solutions with all partners,
more patience, an adapted remuneration.

Bpifrance is subject to State aid regulations (guarantee funds, research, development and innovation, subsidised loans)
which prohibit in particular the financing of companies in proven difficulty or those that are not up to date with their social
and tax contributions.

1.1.2. Guarantee

Directly correlated to the financing granted by its partners, Bpifrance's guarantee is a crucial tool for encouraging banks
to finance SMEs in the riskiest phases of their growth, especially in the creation, innovation or transmission phases.
Bpifrance provides guarantees for bank financing (including leasing and financial leases) and support from equity
investors:

• with regard to creation;

• with regard to innovation ;

• with regard to development;

• with regard to environment;

• with regard to transfers;

• with regard to strengthening the cash flow;

• with regard to international actions, the risk of failure of French subsidiaries abroad (GPI).

The standard loan-to-value ratio is between 40% and 60%. As part of a recovery plan, the guarantee ratio is increased
to between 60 and 70% on the Development, Creation and Transmission funds. The guarantee can reach 80% in
cooperation with the guarantee funds set up by the Regions with Bpifrance Régions..

The guarantee is the preferred tool for intervention, particularly with the smallest companies, as it is implemented in
partnership with private banks, which are authorised to decide immediately on granting up to 200,000 euros, or by
requesting Bpifrance for higher amounts.

The student loan guarantee, a scheme carried out on behalf of the State and greatly strengthened under the recovery
plan, makes it possible to guarantee loans made by banks for students' benefit without the latter having to provide other
guarantees.

1.1.4.1 • Tasks of the Investment Division Business Line

I.

Tasks of the Investment Division business line

As part of the creation of Bpifrance, the investment doctrine was adopted on 25 June 2013 by the Board of Directors of
Bpifrance. This doctrine, which applies to all of the Investment division carried of Bpifrance (and therefore to Bpifrance
Investissement), is summarized as follows:

Through its equity interventions, Bpifrance finances the development and growth of VSBs and SMEs, and aims at the
emergence, consolidation and multiplication of mid-caps, an essential link to the competitiveness of the French economy
and the development of exports.

Bpifrance's investments, in funds as well as in companies, are carried out selectively, in accordance with good
professional practices, depending on the value creation potential (for the investor and for the national economy) of the
companies or funds financed. However, Bpifrance is not an investor like any other. Its character as an informed investor
operating under market conditions in the service of collective interest leads him to complete the investment offer of
market segments characterised by a lack of private funds. As a result, Bpifrance devotes a significant part of its equity
investments, from its own resources and those of third-party investors managed by Bpifrance, to the seed investment,
venture capital, development capital and transfer capital segments, or to funds geared towards profitable companies in
the social and solidarity economy but which, due in particular to their status, naturally attract few traditional investors.
Bpifrance is also working to develop a mezzanine fund offer on the market as a complement or substitute for equity
investments for companies whose shareholders would not like to open the capital to third-party investors.

In this context, all operations in which Bpifrance is involved are guided by the following principles:

• Bpifrance intervenes in order to create, via minority shareholdings, a spillover effect of private investment
through public investment. Whether it invests its own resources or those of other public or private subscribers
under its management, Bpifrance systematically seeks private co-investors to whom it leaves the majority of
shares, in order to stimulate the investment market;

• Bpifrance is an informed investor operating under market conditions. When it co-invests, Bpifrance intervenes
according to the same financial and legal provisions as the co-investors (pari passu). Wherever possible, it sits
on the boards of directors of the companies in which it has invested and on the advisory and strategic
committees of partner funds;

• Bpifrance is a patient investor. It adapts its investment horizon, particularly to the technological context of the
company, and can accept that the profitability of its investments will materialise over a longer time horizon than
most private investors. This is particularly the case in high-risk market segments (start-up, venture capital, R&D
intensive). It supports the companies in which it invests over the long term, which does not exclude rotation of
the asset portfolio in synergy with private co-investors, with a view to good risk management, freeing up room
for manoeuvre to finance new investments and increasing the value of its assets;

1.1.4.2 • How the various investment business lines operate

Bpifrance’s investment activity is based on two different operating methods: direct investment and indirect (or fund of
funds) investment.

• Direct investment whereby the Bpifrance Investment teams themselves:

•

appraise projects to invest in the capital of target firms (carry out due diligence, negotiate entry values
and the terms of shareholders’ agreements: condition of representation on the governing body, any exit
clauses, etc.),

• Execute the investment transactions,

• Subsequently monitor the participating interests (participation in governance bodies, any reinvestments,

etc.) until their disposal;

•

Indirect investment whereby the Bpifrance Investment teams appraise projects and make subscriptions
that they subsequently monitor throughout their lifetime, not in companies, but in funds managed by
private management teams who, in turn, will invest directly in the companies.

• A second key difference of the investment business line as it operates at Bpifrance Investissement, whether

directly or indirectly, is that in terms of investing in capital, Bpifrance acts:

•

•

either on its own behalf: thus exposing its own balance sheet;

or on behalf of a third party: in this instance, Bpifrance is solely the manager of the resources entrusted
to it by other actors.

The majority (39.1 billion euros of the 53.7 billion euros in assets under management as at 30 June 2024) of Bpifrance’s
equity investments are therefore made from the group’s own capital held by Bpifrance Participations. A minority share
(14.6 billion euros as at 30 June 2024), which is nevertheless significant and has been growing in recent years (3.5
billion euros at the end of 2015), of equity investment is made from resources that have been entrusted to Bpifrance by
public or private investors (limited partners or LPs). Historically, since 2010, the French State, through the PIA (and
subsequently France 2030) equity programmes, has been the main provider of funds for this activity. More recently, the
share of private LPs (insurers, industrialists, sovereign funds, the EIF) has been rising, in particular since the creation
of the Lac 1 fund in 2020.

Calculation of expected credit losses

Update of the forward-looking scenarios

Details of the scenarios

In June 2024, Bpifrance updated the four forward-looking scenarios.

These scenarios take into account several constraints on the French economy,
and in particular the continuing significant difficulties posed by recruitment. After
the sluggish growth seen in the first half of 2024, it is assumed that consumption
and investment will gradually pick up during the coming quarters, in a context in
which it is expected that disinflation will continue and the ECB will gradually relax
its monetary policy. The reduction in the factors restricting demand is expected
to endure into 2025 and to translate into an upturn in activity. The context that is
relatively less conducive to a relaxation in sovereign rates due, in particular, to
the uncertain trajectory of public finances and US monetary policy, which is
restrictive in the longer-term, is also taken into account.

Uncertainties about growth prospects remain high. The optimistic scenario
anticipates more dynamic growth in 2025, due to a more rapid easing of price
pressures, implying more dynamic consumption and a more rapid normalisation
of monetary policy. Conversely, the degraded inflationary scenario with bond
crash envisages a shock that is both recessionary and inflationary due to high
and longer lasting pressures on supply and on the price of inputs. The effects of
this shock are assumed to be enduring due to a higher number of business
failures and sharper deterioration on the labour market. The degraded L-shaped
scenario assumes a strong recessive shock on GDP of a health, financial or
cyclical nature, which should lead to a drop in inflation.

The scenarios also take account of the economic consequences of the ecological
and energy transition (EET), which are assumed to translate into a negative
supply shock, at least temporarily, impacting potential growth and sustaining
inflation over the whole forecast period (2024-2029); the negative impact on
potential growth would be the highest in the downgraded scenarios due to a
disorderly transition.

Change in weightings
In the absence of marked changes in the economic hypotheses over the first half
of 2024, the weightings for the scenarios from 31 December 2023 have been
retained.

Correlation between growth rate and default rate
The health crisis context of the years 2020 and 2021 required the application of
a methodological adjustment to the Bpifrance ECL (Expected Credit Losses)
calculation model, to correct the observed decorrelation between the economic
context and the default rates.
Bpifrance does not include the data from 2020 to 2023 in the calibration of the
ECL calculation model as at 30 June 2024, and continues to use the parameters
used at the end of 2019.
The effect of not recalibrating the model is a reduction in ECL of €29m as at 30
June 2024 (-€45m as at 31 December 2023).

Correlation between inflation rate and default rate
The economic context of 2022, 2023 and to a lesser extent 2024 translated into
sharply rising inflation forecasts, at levels well above the values present in the
calibration history of the models. The correlation observed historically in
Bpifrance portfolios between inflation and default is negative: rising inflation is
associated with a decrease in defaults.
Therefore, updating this projected inflation has a strong counter-intuitive effect in
the results of the model, as it would result in a large decrease in the calculated
amount of ECL. This result is explained by the change in the current inflation
regime compared with the last ten years and does not correspond to the expected
effects of this shock on French business failures.
To mitigate this effect, Bpifrance has decided to neutralise the effect of inflation
in the update of the ECLs, by retaining in its macroeconomic scenarios the
inflation trajectories defined at 31 December 2021.
The effect of this neutralisation of inflation effects is an increase in the ECL of
€60m at 30 June 2024 (€99m at 31 December 2023).

Correction to the forward-looking model
A new version of the provisioning model has been developed. This will enable
improvements to the model’s ability to process the data from the pandemic
period, as well as its reaction in high-inflation contexts. This model had not yet
been used to calculate the ECL as at 30 June 2024, but the impact of its planned
roll-out in the second half of 2024 is known and translates to an increase in the
ECL calculated of in the order of €90m.
An overlay of €90m has therefore been included in the ECL as at 30 June 2024
to take account of this improvement to the model and in anticipation of the
foreseeable impact of its roll-out in the second half of 2024.

Sector-based adjustments

Inflationary shock
Due to the recognition of an overlay in relation to the new forward-looking
modelling, this adjustment allowing the inflationary context to be taken into
consideration has been eliminated. It accounted for an increase in the ECL of
€43m as at 31 December 2023.

Construction
The construction sector is characterised by a high level of failures and a degraded
context with, in particular, a reduction in the number of construction permits and
works commenced.
In order to incorporate this sector-based adjustment in the provisioning as at 30
June 2024, the businesses in this sector have been downgraded by one rank
when calculating the ECL.
This translates to an increase in the ECL of €4.5m.

Other forward-looking adjustments

Innovation
The population of innovative businesses was characterised by high levels of
failure in 2023 and the first half of 2024 in a context in which fundraising has
become rarer.
A certain number of these businesses depend on fundraising to finance their
growth and continue their activities. This tendency raises the risk of default for
this population.
A provision adjustment was recognised in the ECL as at 30 June 2024, through
the identification of a population of businesses with negative self-financing ability
and insufficient cashflow to finance a new year of activity without external
resources. The loans affected were placed in bucket 2.
This adjustment amounted to €19m as at 31 December 2023. Its update, using
the same methodology, led to a provision adjustment of €20m as at 30 June 2024.

Firms with a State-guaranteed loan (SGL)
The cash flow of French companies has been massively supported since the
spring of 2020 through the distribution of state-guaranteed loans. These loans
included a one-year grace period, which could be extended for an optional year
thereafter. Consequently, the bulk of SGLs have been in the payback period since
2022 or 2023. This debt burden poses difficulties for some businesses that took
out an SGL.
Therefore, the population of businesses that took out an SGL and whose financial
statements show that they have insufficient available resources to repay one year
of this loan have been identified. An adjustment to the ECL has been recognised
in addition to the results obtained from the models as at 30 June 2024 for this
population. The corresponding loans have been placed in bucket 2.
This adjustment was for €40m as at 31 December 2023. Its update, using the
same methodology, leads to a sum of €34m as at 30 June 2024.

Real estate valuation
A significant proportion of Bpifrance’s outstanding amounts is guaranteed by real
estate assets affected by the tertiary sector decree (décret tertiaire). This decree
imposes obligations aimed at improving the energy performance of buildings, as
well as obligations to disclose current consumption. The data available shows
that a significant number of businesses have not made the mandatory
disclosures, which is a reflection of insufficient efforts to initiate the energy
transition of these real estate assets. This poses a long-term valuation risk for the
affected assets. A post-model adjustment of €80m was applied as at 30 June
2024, the same as at 31 December 2023, to take account of this risk.

The context for the year is uncertain in terms of the valuation of commercial real
estate as transaction and investment volumes are falling. The segments deemed
to be the most affected are offices, shops (excluding major food retailers) and
health. A post-model adjustment of €20m was applied to these sectors when
calculating the ECL as at 30 June 2024, the same as at 31 December 2023, for
this valuation risk for commercial real estate.

These two post-model adjustments are made on account of risk related to loss in
the event of default and not to the likelihood of default. They have no effect on
the assessment of the significant degradation of the credit risk and do not lead to
changes in the bucket classification of the outstanding amounts.
