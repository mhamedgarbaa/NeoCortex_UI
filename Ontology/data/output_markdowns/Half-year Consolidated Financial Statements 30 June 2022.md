1. BUSINESS ACTIVITIES

1.1. Presentation of the missions and business lines

A public group serving the financing and development of businesses, Bpifrance acts in support of the
policies conducted by the State and the Regions. The law No. 2012-1559 of 31 December 2012 tasks it
with promoting innovation, start-up, development, internationalisation, transfer and transmission of
businesses, by contributing to their financing through loans and equity.

Bpifrance is a credit institution and a holding company that acts through its subsidiaries, Bpifrance Régions
(which operates the Financing activities, together with the credit institution), Bpifrance Participations,
Bpifrance Investissement and Bpifrance Assurance Export).

As a credit institution, Bpifrance is subject to banking regulations (Order of 3 November 2014 on the internal
control of companies in the banking, payment services and investment services sector subject to the
supervision of the Prudential Supervisory and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de
résolution)), which imposes numerous obligations on it, such as operating under market conditions, without
abusive support or ruinous credit, with a risk prevention system (financial, image and operational),
separation of roles, monitoring measurement systems and risk control. Bpifrance and its subsidiary
Bpifrance Régions are supervised by the Prudential Supervisory and Resolution Authority and subject to
direct prudential supervision by the European Central Bank1.

Bpifrance is a neutral player in the marketplace, whose role is to bring together all financing partners for
the benefit of companies, banks first and foremost, as well as innovation development networks by co-
financing with private banks.

Bpifrance provides financing to companies in their riskiest phases, such as business creation or transfer,
innovation, international development, as well as in the context of capacity investments, while respecting
the rules of competition law and private players. To do this, it relies on allocations from public actors at
different levels: State, local authorities (especially the Regions) and the European Union.

Its public interest mission requires Bpifrance to intervene as close as possible to the territories, in favour of
VSEs, SMEs and intermediate-sized enterprises, regardless of their legal status (including SSE companies2
and local public companies3 performing a commercial activity), in all sectors of activity, except financial
services.

- For transmission;
- For reinforcement of the cash flow;
- For international business, including bank guarantees on export markets and the risk of failure

Bpifrance's investments in funds and companies are made selectively, in accordance with good
professional practice, on the basis of the value creation potential (for the investor and for the national
economy) of the companies or funds financed. However, Bpifrance is not an investor like any other. Its
character as a prudent investor operating under market conditions in the service of the collective interest
leads it to complete the investment offer in market segments characterised by a lack of private funds. As a
result, Bpifrance devotes a significant portion of its equity investments, from its own resources and those
of third-party investors it manages, to the start-up, venture capital, development capital and capital-
transmission segments, or in funds oriented towards profitable companies in the social economy but which,
due to their status, spontaneously attract few traditional investors. Bpifrance is also committed to developing
a mezzanine fund offer on the market as a complement or substitute for equity investments for companies
whose shareholders do not wish to open up their capital to third-party investors.

In this context, all operations in which Bpifrance is involved are guided by the following principles:

 Bpifrance intervenes in order to create, via minority shareholdings, a spill over effect of private
investment through public investment. Whether it invests its own resources or those of other public or
private subscribers under its management, Bpifrance systematically seeks private co-investors to whom
it leaves the majority of shares, in order to stimulate the investment market;

 Bpifrance is an informed investor operating under market conditions. When it co-invests, Bpifrance
intervenes according to the same financial and legal provisions as the co-investors (pari passu).
Wherever possible, it sits on the boards of directors of the companies in which it has invested and on
the advisory and strategic committees of partner funds;

 Bpifrance is a patient investor. It adapts its investment horizon, particularly to the technological context
of the company, and can accept that the profitability of its investments will materialise over a longer time
horizon than most private investors. This is particularly the case in high-risk market segments (start-up,
venture capital, R&D intensive). It supports the companies in which it invests over the long term, which
does not exclude rotation of the asset portfolio in synergy with private co-investors, with a view to good
risk management, freeing up room for manoeuvre to finance new investments and increasing the value
of its assets;

 Bpifrance is at the service of the collective interest. In addition to the necessary assessment of the
financial performance of companies (profitability, sustainability, liquidity), its investment decisions are
made with regard to the impact of projects on the competitiveness of the French economy, assessed
using a multi-criteria analysis grid, integrating extra-financial criteria such as export and international
development potential, contribution to innovation, ESG (environmental, social and corporate
governance) practices, effects on employment and regional development, role in the sector,
development of family businesses, etc.

 In order to ensure that its investments effectively contribute to the growth and development of
companies, Bpifrance gives priority to bringing new money to the companies in which it invests,
alongside other investors.

3. THE RISKS

3.1. Credit risk

As a banking institution, Bpifrance is exposed to credit risk on its corporate financing transactions. This
includes:

•

default risk: the risk that a creditor will permanently lose its claim to the extent that the debtor
cannot, even by liquidating all its assets, repay all its commitments;

•

• migration risk: the potential loss resulting from the downgrading (or rating downgrade) of an
internal/external credit instrument or counterparty, as well as the potential indirect losses that
may result from a credit migration event;
single name concentration risk: the risk arising from exposure to counterparties considered to
be the same group of related clients
sector concentration risk: the risk arising from exposure to counterparties operating in the
same economic sector.

•

Bpifrance mainly deploys its financing activities for French companies of all sizes (from very small
businesses to large corporations), through a wide range of asset classes (debt financing, loans, guarantees)
and commitment horizons (short to long term). In the financing that it grants, Bpifrance ensures that risk-
taking is limited by means of (i) the implementation of co-financing involving risk-sharing with a commercial
bank, (ii) the implementation of collateral or guarantees.

Bpifrance Group Half-year Consolidated Financial Statements at 30 June 2022

Bpifrance manages its individual and global credit risk exposures based on a granting policy that defines
the characteristics of credit files and the levels of delegation according to the size of the commitment or
the sensitive and/or specific nature of the transaction. Credit decisions are based on a synthetic,
comprehensive and regularly updated analysis, the Decision Support Tool and the Fiben ratings
provided by the Banque de France. They are made on the basis of objective criteria, with a long-term
perspective, a desire for diversification, and by anticipating and taking into account changes in client
ecosystems. These decisions are monitored and reviewed on a regular basis. The Bpifrance Group is
particularly vigilant about risk concentration (by client, sector, rating) and is careful to maintain a
diversified portfolio.

All Bpifrance commitments (financing, guarantee and innovation operations) involving credit risks are
subject to an initial analysis by the Financing and Network Management Department, which is
responsible for monitoring and client relations. The Credit Risk Department is responsible for the
counter-analysis of files that fall within the jurisdiction of the Credit Risk Committee and the Credit
Committee of Bpifrance, and presents them to these committees for decision.

On the basis of IFRS standards, at 30 June 2022, Bpifrance’s maximum exposure to credit risk was
€89,423.7 million, compared with €87,153.1 million at 31 December 2021.

3.2. Liquidity risk

The liquidity risk corresponds with the bank’s inability to meet its obligations at an acceptable price, for
a given location and currency. This risk can occur in case of non-concomitance of the cash flows. The
refinancing risk (an integral part of the liquidity risk) arises when the funds needed to finance the non-
liquid assets cannot be obtained within acceptable timeframes and at acceptable prices.

The Group’s liquidity risk is monitored as part of a liquidity risk management policy defined in the Group
Risk Management Policy approved by the Board of Directors. The liquidity situation of the Bpifrance
Group is assessed on the basis of internal standards, alarm indicators and regulatory ratios..

The system in place, specifically defined as part of the ILAAP (Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment
Process), provides Bpifrance with the necessary liquidity to meet its short-term and medium/long-term
liquidity obligations. These measures should enable Bpifrance to fulfil its regulatory obligations.

3.3. Liquidity, interest rate, and currency risks

The interest rate risk is the risk that Bpifrance may incur losses due to an unfavourable change in interest
rates, particularly in the event of an imbalance between the interest rates generated by its assets and
those due on its liabilities. Bpifrance generates a portion of its earnings in the form of net interest margin
and as such remains highly exposed to interest rate fluctuations and changes in the yield curve,
particularly in its Financing activities.

Bpifrance is exposed to foreign exchange risk, which is the risk that changes in currency exchange rates
will adversely affect the value of the instruments and therefore generate losses on capital borrowed or
loaned in currencies other than the euro.

The Bpifrance Group manages its balance sheet in such a way that its structure is globally balanced in
terms of rates and exchange rates. The backing sought between usages and resources is intended to
limit the establishment’s exposure to financial risks. The overall rate and exchange risks are measured
each month, and governed by a system of limits. The rate and exchange risks are hedged by means of
future financial instruments negotiated on organised or over-the-counter markets (primarily interest rate
and currency swap contracts), or by means of operations involving State securities. The ALM Committee
examines interest rate and foreign exchange risks and also ensures compliance with the limits set. In
addition to these periodic meetings, the ALM Committee may be convened, in particular if market
parameters change abruptly or if the limits set are exceeded.

3.4. Risks generated by the investment activity

As part of its investment activity, the Bpifrance group holds significant securities portfolios composed
mainly of listed and unlisted shares, as well as units in investment funds. It is exposed to the risk of
impairment of its listed and unlisted investments. In addition, the structure of the portfolio of the
subsidiary Bpifrance Participations exposes it to the variability of the fair value of the investments, which
may result in a direct loss of capital.

The constitution of these portfolios is part of the implementation of the Group’s investment policy, which
favours minority shareholdings, operated in a prudent approach and with a “patient” capital approach.

No Group equity portfolio is dedicated to trading. Bpifrance does not invest more than the amount of its
own funds and closely monitors the position risk related to the impairment of investments in direct or
indirect shareholdings of the Group.

Bpifrance strives to contain, at all times, the risk of loss of market value in the event of severe stress.

3.5. Operational risk

The operational risk of the Bpifrance group is the risk of loss resulting from faulty or inadequate internal
processes (particularly those involving staff and information systems) or from external events, whether
deliberate, accidental or natural (floods, fires, earthquakes, terrorist attacks, etc.). Operational risk
includes fraud, human resources risks, legal and reputational risks, compliance risks, information
systems risks, inappropriate outsourced service provision, as well as risks of business process failures
including credit processes, or the use of a model (model risk).

The Bpifrance Group has an operational risk governance and management system, the main purpose
of which is to ensure the overall control of operational and non-compliance risks and to provide
reasonable assurance that the objectives set by the Group are achieved. The framework related to this
system is defined in the Bpifrance Internal Control Charter, which constitutes the benchmark for internal
control within the Group. Bpifrance's Operational Risk Management Policy is applied in strict compliance
with regulatory obligations and is aligned with the classification of operational risks established by the
Basel Committee (Article 324 of European Parliament Regulation 575/2013). At the level of the second
line of defence, the Compliance and Permanent Control Department, and more specifically the
Operational Risk Department within it, is responsible for the organisation and functioning of the
operational risk and non-compliance control system.

3.6. Non-compliance risk

The risk of non-compliance is the risk of legal, administrative or disciplinary sanction, significant financial
loss or damage to reputation, arising from non-compliance with directly applicable national or European
legislative or regulatory provisions specific to banking and financial activities, professional and ethical
standards, or instructions issued in application of the guidelines of the supervisory body.

Bpifrance ensures that it (i) complies with all laws and regulations, particularly those relating to banking
and finance, to which it is subject and (ii) establishes solid and effective relations with its supervisory
authorities.

The Compliance and Permanent Control Department is responsible for supervising the system for
controlling non-compliance risks. To this end, the Compliance and Permanent Control Department
steers the procedures, systems and controls implemented by the group's companies to ensure that the
Bpifrance Group's operations comply with the laws, regulations and rules of the market, particularly with
regard to banking and financial regulations.

More specifically, Bpifrance has deployed a system to combat financial crime and thus prevent money
laundering, terrorist financing and fraud. Procedures relating to combating money laundering and
terrorist financing (AML/CFT) have been put in place; they notably involve dedicated training for all
employees (managers and operational staff) on these issues. A tool dedicated to AML/CFT makes it
possible, in particular, to enter information relating to customers, to file documents relating to "Know
Your Customer" (KYC) and to make the data and links between the various client companies more
reliable. A supplier evaluation system has also been put in place.

3.7. Climate-related risks

Climate-related risks can be divided into two main categories:

• Physical risk refers to the financial consequences that result from climate change through

extreme and/or chronic hazards;

• Transition risk is the risk of losses incurred, directly or indirectly, as a result of inadequate

adaptation to a low-carbon economy.

The Bpifrance Group is gradually integrating the assessment of these risks into its risk management
system. The monitoring of climate risks is indeed a particular challenge for Bpifrance in the context of
its strategic objective of "Green Bank" and the Climate Plan launched in 2020.

Started in 2021, the climate risk management system is being extended to all of Bpifrance's business
lines and will continue to be developed in 2022 in accordance with the action plan produced by the
Group Risk Management Department, including the establishment of a Climate and Environmental Risk
Management Committee.

Bpifrance has developed a framework to assess and quantify the exposure of its portfolio to physical
and climate transition risks. This monitoring framework is based on a series of internal indicators that
are being developed to identify pockets and/or counterparties at risk that may require enhanced analysis
and/or appropriate intervention, and to measure and monitor the sensitivity of the portfolio to transition
and physical risk.

Note 2 - EVENTS DURING THE PERIOD

2.1 Significant events during the period

2.1.1. War in Ukraine

Russia's invasion of Ukraine - on 24 February 2022 - has led to instability in the financial
markets and the wider economy, as well as the unprecedented implementation of new
regulations on economic sanctions by the international community.

Because of its vocation to primarily finance French companies, the Bpifrance group's
direct exposure to Russia, Ukraine and Belarus is very limited and low, of the order of
€5m relating to the net risk of exposures linked to export credits financing Ukrainian
buyers in the context of the support provided by Bpifrance to French exporters.
Of the three equity investments made by Bpifrance Participations in Russian entities,
which have not yet been targeted by French, European or American economic sanctions,
but which were made alongside a Russian partner currently targeted by European and
American economic sanctions, two of them were sold in the second quarter of 2022 and
a sale agreement is being finalised for the third. This position has been fully impaired in
the balance sheet of the Bpifrance group at 30 June 2022.

The impact of this crisis on Bpifrance's client loan portfolio is assessed through the
calculation of expected losses. The effect on growth and the macroeconomic outlook is
taken into account in updating the macroeconomic scenarios used in the forward-looking
information. The effect of supply difficulties, raw material costs and energy costs is
incorporated through adjustments.

In addition, it should be recalled that the Bpifrance Group includes Bpifrance Assurance
Export, which acts in the name of, on behalf of and under the exclusive control of the
French State with regard to the support of French exports. As such, this subsidiary is
involved in the negotiation, implementation and monitoring of this public support for
French exports. These exposures, directly borne by the State (and not on Bpifrance's
balance sheet), represent a little over €560m for Ukraine and around €1bn for Russia.

2.1.2. Calculation of expected credit losses

Financial years 2020 and 2021 were marked by the unprecedented health crisis of Covid-
19. In 2022, the economic and social repercussions of the pandemic, particularly due to
supply difficulties, have been accentuated by the war in Ukraine.

In the first half of 2022, the Bpifrance group recorded a net allocation of €95 million in
cost of risk, which breaks down into an allocation for proven risk of €43 million (bucket
3) and an allocation for expected risk of €52 million (buckets 1 and 2).

Update of the forward-looking scenarios

Details of the scenarios
Since 30 June 2020, Bpifrance has used four forward looking scenarios for the
calculation of the ECLs. These scenarios were updated in June 2022. The downgraded
and severely downgraded scenarios have been replaced by an inflationary downgraded
scenario with a bond crash and an L-shaped downgraded scenario, respectively.

Because Bpifrance’s clients are solely French companies, the scenarios defined concern
French macroeconomic indicators only. The indicators used are the GDP growth rate,
changes in the consumer price index, the unemployment rate, and one-year and ten-
year French treasury bond (OAT) rates.

The scenarios take into account the impact of multiple supply difficulties on the French
economy. In particular, it is assumed that oil and gas prices will be persistently high, due
to a market balance altered by the embargo on Russian products, and that there will be
sustained pressure on the prices of some non-energy commodities. Thus, inflation may
be high throughout 2022 and could weigh on purchasing power, despite economic policy
measures aimed at limiting the consequences of these tensions on both household
consumption and the financial situation of companies. In this context, activity may slow
down in 2022 and the unemployment rate could increase very slightly. The "France
Relance" and "France 2030" plans could nevertheless continue to support activity in
2022 and in the medium term.

With inflation persistently higher than previously expected, the ECB's monetary policy
normalisation could take place earlier than expected at the end of 2021, putting upward
pressure on bond yields, allowing inflation to gradually return to a level close to its 2%
target.

Uncertainties about growth prospects remain high. The optimistic scenario anticipates
more dynamic growth in 2022 due to a rapid decline in input prices and consequently in
inflation, thus limiting the pressure on purchasing power. Conversely, the downside
scenario (inflationary with bond crash) envisages more violent and long-lasting tensions
on supplies and prices, leading to greater stress on sovereign rates. Moreover, the
consequences on the French productive fabric would be persistent, which would strongly
limit the economy's capacity to rebound and lead to a more marked deterioration of the
labour market. The alternative L-shaped scenario assumes a strong recessionary shock
of a health, financial or cyclical nature, followed by stagnation due to a lasting impact on
French production capacity, resulting in a fall in inflation and permanently low interest
rates.

The weightings of the June 2022 scenarios have been changed from the previous year.
The choice of these weightings is largely based on that of the CDC Group.

Methodological adjustments to the model

Correlation between growth rate and default rate
The health crisis context of the years 2020 and 2021 required the application of a
methodological adjustment to the Bpifrance ECL (Expected Credit Losses) calculation
model, to correct the observed decorrelation between the economic context and the
default rates.

Bpifrance does not include the data from 2020 and 2021 in the calibration of the ECL
calculation model at 30 June 2022, and continues to use the parameters used at the end
of 2019.
The effect of not recalibrating the model is an increase in ECL of €47m.

Correlation between inflation rate and default rate
The international context at the beginning of 2022 translates into sharply rising inflation
projections, at levels well above the values present in the calibration history of the
models. The correlation observed historically in Bpifrance portfolios between inflation
and default is negative: rising inflation is associated with a decrease in defaults.
Therefore, updating this projected inflation has a strong counter-intuitive effect in the
results of the model, as it would result in a large decrease in the calculated amount of
ECL. This result is explained by the change in the current inflation regime compared with
the last ten years and does not correspond to the expected effects of this shock on
French business failures.
To mitigate this effect, Bpifrance has decided to neutralise the effect of inflation in the
update of the ECLs, by retaining in its macroeconomic scenarios the inflation trajectories
defined at 31 December 2021.

The effect of this neutralisation of inflation effects is an increase in the ECL of €159m.

Sector-based adjustments

Tourism and leisure
Tourism and leisure professionals are among those most affected by the COVID-19
pandemic. As such, this sector was subject to expert adjustment of internal ratings in the
calculation of ECLs. Activity in the sector has now returned to a very satisfactory level,
and the effects of the 2020 crisis are incorporated into the companies' financial
statements on which the internal rating is based.
In this context, the expert adjustment of the tourism sector ratings has been removed for
the calculation of ECLs at 30 June 2022; this adjustment amounted to €27m at 31
December 2021.

Industry
The significant tensions on supplies that appeared during 2021 continued and amplified
in 2022 due to the international context. These difficulties concern both raw materials,
components and transport. These tensions are considered to be long-lasting (with a
horizon of more than 12 months) and affect companies in the industry sector, worsening
their financial situation (impact on profitability, cash flow and working capital
requirements).
In order to incorporate the foreseeable effects on the solvency of the companies
concerned, an adjustment is applied to companies in the industry sector. This adjustment
increases ECL by €44 million.

Other forward-looking adjustments

The cash flow of French companies has been massively supported since the spring of
2020 through the distribution of state-guaranteed loans. These loans included a one-
year grace period, which could be extended for an optional year thereafter. As a result,
the bulk of the state-guaranteed loans enter the amortization period in the second and
third quarters of 2022. This increase in the debt burden after two years of deferred
repayment could put some companies that have benefited from a state-guaranteed loan
in difficulty.
In order to take into account this risk on the repayment capacity of the companies, an
adjustment to the ECL of €90 million has been made in addition to the results of the
models, an amount which remains unchanged at 30 June 2022.

Measurement of the significant increase in credit risk

Bpifrance uses the following significant credit risk increase criteria to allocate its
exposures to the various buckets:
- Non-performing exposures are classified in bucket 3;
- Performing exposures are classified in bucket 2 if they meet one of the following criteria:

o The counterparty is placed on a watch list;
o The counterparty’s annualised probability of default at maturity on the
calculation date is greater than 20%;
o The counterparty's annualised probability of default on the calculation date
has increased by over 2% relative to the probability of default on the decision
date, and the increase in the annualised probability of default at maturity is
greater than 95% of the probability of default on the decision date.

- Performing exposures that do not meet any of the above conditions are classified in
bucket 1.

Ordinarily, Bpifrance takes advantage of the possibility afforded by the standard to use
the one-year probability of default as a measure of a significant increase in credit risk as
an approximation of the probability of default at maturity.

In the context of the health crisis, which was marked by a significant rebound in activity
following a very sharp economic shock, the probability of default at one year could not
be considered a good approximation of the probability of default over the entire lifetime
of the credit. To overcome this problem, the determination of whether credit risk has
significantly increased had been adjusted by adding a lifetime dimension to the
probability of default. This probability of default is compared to the one-year probability
of default at the origin of the instrument. The absolute and relative thresholds used in
