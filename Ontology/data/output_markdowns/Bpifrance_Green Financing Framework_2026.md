A brief overview of Bpifrance

Bpifrance acts as a Development Bank, Innovation Agency, Sovereign Wealth Fund, and
Export Credit Agency. Its general interest mission leads Bpifrance to promote the financing
and the development of companies operating in France, in particular SMEs. Bpifrance is a
public group created by the law of 12 July 2013. It aims to finance and develop companies
and acts in accordance with the public policies conducted by both the French State and
French regional authorities. Bpifrance has a highly protected legal status and is closely
supervised by the public authorities: the French State, through EPIC (1) Bpifrance (49.18 %)
and the Caisse des Dépôts (49.18  %), owns and controls Bpifrance. Bpifrance’s “raison
d’être” reflects the 2019 “loi PACTE” which encourages companies to “include social and
environmental issues related to their activity in their articles of association”.

Bpifrance global sustainable strategy

General and public interest
Since its foundation, Bpifrance has held the belief that finance should serve general and
public interests. This belief was formalised by French parliamentarians at Bpifrance’s
creation in 2012, when its purpose and missions were defined with a focus on ESG issues.

Bpifrance’s mission is also to develop and stimulate the French economy by financing the
development and innovation of French Micro and SMEs (MSMEs). By supporting employment
generation and preservation, by encouraging female entrepreneurship and by helping
companies in disadvantaged areas, Bpifrance is driven by economic, environmental, and
social challenges.

4 societal priorities - aligned with the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) have
been set by Bpifrance to constitute pivotal points of its commitments:

• Support employment and job creation

• Quality of Governance & Management

• Encouraging women’s entrepreneurship

• Ecological and Energy transition

Each of Bpifrance’s activities is aligned with at least one Sustainable Development Goal (SDG).

As such, Bpifrance aims to contribute in addressing the SDG challenges (4) faced by the
French State as identified in the Sustainable Development Report. Further, Bpifrance
annually conducts an impact assessment, with a 3-year look-back period, to assess its
direct economic, social and environmental impact.

A 360° view of Bpifrance Corporate Social Responsibility and ESG issues integration
As an investor: a responsible investor policy (5) is applied to operations in all activities.
Bpifrance systematically takes into account extra-financial ESG criteria which are likely to
have an impact on investment decisions. ESG assessments are integrated throughout the
life cycle of each investment. Furthermore, in the due diligence phase, investment teams
use an ESG assessment tool to identify potential ESG issues. This tool has been developed to
be sector and size sensitive. It allows Bpifrance investors to assess ESG issues’ materiality
related to the activity of the investee company. The ability of the investee company to
face and respond to such issues is also taken into account. Additional due diligence could
be conducted with the management to establish an improvement strategy, if needed, as
stated in Bpifrance Responsible Shareholder Engagement policy. (6)

As a financer: Bpifrance conducts a 360-degree analysis of its customer’s climate strategy.
The Climate Maturity Index evaluates the exposure and maturity of companies in relation to
the challenges of the green transition.

The transition risk analyses have been reinforced for exposed sectors, including the
transportation, real estate, construction and building sectors. Those sectors are monitored
by internal expert groups to establish best-in-class procedures on evaluation and analysis
before financing.

During the credit granting process, account managers conduct due diligence on the
physical climate risks to which borrowing entities are exposed, using an internal tool called
Climate Risk Management (CRM). The hazards analyzed include drought, shrink-swell
behavior of clay soils, heatwaves, floods, and wildfires. These risks are assessed under
three IPCC climate scenarios (RCP 2.6, 4.5, and 8.5) and across three-time horizons (short-
term, medium-term, and long-term).

The CRM tool is designed to: (i) assess the levels of physical climate risks affecting the
locations of a group or company, and (ii) carry out a complementary analysis in cases where
high risk is identified for at least one hazard. This includes verifying the risk mitigation
measures implemented by the group, company, or the municipality where the entity is
located, covering financial, human, technical, and organizational aspects for companies, or
administrative aspects for municipalities.

As an export credit insurer: environmental and social aspects are included in project
analysis for Bpifrance Assurance Export guarantees over €10m or located in sensitive
areas, following OECD’s ESG due diligence recommendations (7). Since January 2021,
Bpifrance and the French State propose preferential export guarantee conditions to
sustainable projects, in line with EU Taxonomy.

As an employer: the QLW (8) agreement addresses work conditions, focusing on health,
stress, psychosocial risks, harassment, and violence at work. Some specific health
measures to protect employees undertaken in 2020 have been reconducted. A training
course for new managers is set up to prevent psychosocial risks. Bpifrance has signed the
Charter of Diversity, to fight all form of discrimination. A Gender Equality Agreement has
been put in place. A new disability agreement was signed in 2020.

As a voice to foster ESG: Bpifrance is stepping up its CSR awareness-raising and training
activities for businesses - in 2024 8,600 advice missions were conducted by Bpifrance’s
experts, and 1,031 new businesses were accelerated. Bpifrance Université offers 45 courses
on CSR and more than 9,500 learners were on the online university platform in 2024. Many
sector-specific guides have been developed to raise awareness about climate, risks and
challenges of energy transition, opportunities, and solutions.

Supporting the production of decarbonized energy: €11bn target by 2029

Access to decarbonized energy is crucial for the green transition, especially in the industrial
sector. Bpifrance plans to provide €11 billion in funding to renewable energy projects
by 2030, facilitating these projects through the issuance of green bonds. Support for
independent developers and producers of renewable energy will involve the mobilization
of Bpifrance’s investment teams. The production of decarbonized energy will also rely
on innovation, new energies, and the nuclear sector (hydrogen, batteries, small modular
reactors, biomass, etc.). Bpifrance will also aim to facilitate access to decarbonized energy
for companies, particularly in the industrial sector.

To ensure access to clean energy, Bpifrance will continue its historical support for
renewable energy production. The bank aims to remain the leading financier of renewable
energy projects in France, while expanding its reach to European and emerging markets.
It will also facilitate long-term energy contracts for industrial consumers and diversify its
financing tools.

Key actions:

• €11bn in financing for renewable energy projects by 2029.

• Deployment of the FIEE2 fund (€240m) to support equity investments in renewable

energy developers.

• Support for Corporate Power Purchase Agreements (CPPAs) and long-term nuclear

energy contracts for SMEs.

• Expansion of financing tools (e.g. revolving credit lines, bridge loans).

• Development of export financing for renewable energy projects.

• Promote export financing for renewable energy projects.

sector will be a priority, with specific actions to reduce emissions by supporting SMEs
electrifying their processes, improving their energy efficiency, eco-design, and circular
economy.

Bpifrance will intensify its support to help SMEs and midcaps reduce their carbon footprint,
particularly in the industrial sector. The bank will deploy diagnostics, advisory services, and
dedicated financing to promote electrification, energy efficiency, and circular economy
practices.

Key actions :

• 20,000 companies to be supported in their green transition.

• 8,000 industrial decarbonization missions, including Diag Décarbon’Action and
Diag Adaptation.

Rationale for Bpifrance Green Financing Framework

In accordance with French Law Loi de Transition Énergétique pour la croissance verte (32)

have to engage in a fast and unprecedent change of its global energy mix, while increasing

and by replacing the fossil fuel-based electricity generation.

Environmental issues are considered in the financing process. During the credit granting
process, account managers conduct due diligence on the physical climate risks to which
borrowing entities are exposed, using an internal tool called Climate Risk Management

2.6, 4.5, and 8.5) and across three time horizons (short-term, medium-term, and long-term).

The CRM tool is designed to: (i) assess the levels of physical climate risks affecting the
locations of a group or company, and (ii) carry out a complementary analysis in cases where
high risk is identified for at least one hazard. This includes verifying the risk mitigation
measures implemented by the group, company, or the municipality where the entity is
located, covering financial, human, technical, and organizational aspects for companies, or
administrative aspects for municipalities.

Moreover, Bpifrance conducts a 360-degree analysis of its customer’s climate strategy.
The Climate Maturity Index evaluates the exposure and maturity of companies in relation
to the challenges of ecological transition. During the financing process, an interview is
conducted with the counterparts to identify the climate ambition of the company and
the measures it takes to reduce its environmental impact. This interview consists of a
generic part and a specific part, which is tailored to the targeted industry. It is intended
to be repeated every two years to monitor the evolution of the funded company’s climate
strategy. The different steps include: (i) Interview and questionnaire between the account
manager and the executive, (ii) Generation of Climate Maturity Index assessment with
strengths and improvements, (iii) Transition support by the account manager, (iv) Ongoing
customer dialogue with the Bpifrance ecosystem.

Climate Maturity Index assessments mainly lead to physical diagnostics with engineering
firms that come to conduct physical measurements and initiate transformation with the
executives. Any new borrower has to prove to Bpifrance the processes in place to reduce
company’s impact. Technical solution on short and medium term has to be disclosed with
a process optimization required, especially on Climate Change Adaptation. Furthermore,
Bpifrance asks the borrower to formalize a change management policy and a detailed
process of the actions listed. Remediation measures has to be set on counterparty side if
any negative impact has been spotted.

Management of Proceeds
The proceeds of the Green Financing Instruments issued under this Framework will be
managed by the Green Financing Committee in a portfolio approach. Bpifrance’s treasury
team will be responsible for the management of the net proceeds of Green Financing
Instruments, to ensure that an amount equal to such proceeds is effectively invested in the
financing and/or re-financing of Eligible Green Loans.

The net proceeds of the Green Financing Instruments will be deposited in Bpifrance’s
general account and an amount equal to the net proceeds will be earmarked for allocation
to Eligible Green Loans (full allocation takes place each calendar year), in accordance with
the principles outlined in this Green Financing Framework. The Eligible Green Loans will
be consolidated in Bpifrance’s balance sheet under the IFRS accounting standards. All
relevant information regarding the issuance of Bpifrance’s Green Financing Instruments
will be monitored and kept into Bpifrance’s internal accounting systems.

Dynamic portfolio approach

Bpifrance will strive to maintain in its portfolio a volume of Eligible Green Loans in excess
of the volume of outstanding Green Financing Instruments at any time.

During the life of the Green Financing Instruments, Eligible Green Loans will be added to
or removed from the Eligible Green Loans Portfolio to the extent required (e.g. in case of
projects divestment or cancellation, in case of amortized or redeemed loans, or if a project
ceases to meet the Eligible Green Loans Criteria). If the total volume of the eligible portfolio
falls below the balance of the outstanding green instruments in circulation, Bpifrance
commits to reaching an equivalent or higher level within 12 months.

Unallocated Proceeds

If any Unallocated Proceeds to Eligible Green Loans exist, they will be managed by Bpifrance’s
treasury team in compliance with the procedures and internal policies regarding liquidity
management, to ensure that the funds are invested in liquid, short-term and certain
investments, with a view to their future allocation. In any case, Unallocated Proceeds will
be temporary. For the sake of clarity Unallocated Proceeds will not be invested in GHG
intensive activities (86) or activities listed in the exclusion list stated in section 2.1 of this
Green Financing Framework.

Bpifrance issues multiple series of Green Financing Instruments.

The internal methods for earmarking and allocating an amount equivalent to the net
proceeds from the issuances of Green Financing Instruments will be reviewed by an
external auditor.

Coupon and final redemption payments

Coupon payments and final redemption of Bpifrance Green Financing Instruments are not
directly linked to any associated Eligible Green Loans, as the interest payments periods
and maturities may not match. Thus, coupons and final redemption payments of Green
Financing Instruments are paid out of Bpifrance’s general liquidity pool at its sole discretion.

Increases and buy-backs

The principal amount of Green Financing Instruments can be increased and further Green
Financing Instruments can be issued, as long as the net proceeds of the relevant issuances
are used for financing and/or refinancing of Eligible Green Loans. Partial or whole buybacks
of Green Financing Instruments are possible to support Bpifrance’s funding programme.
Buybacks can also be financed from Bpifrance’s general liquidity.

Allocation and Impact Reports

Bpifrance will integrate NEUCP and NEUMTN into the annual Allocation and Impact Report,
dedicated section will be created to disclose the maximum amount issued in short-term
and medium-term financing for the year. The allocation reporting will specifically present
the volumes issued in NEUCP and NEUMTN. Information will be shared on the eligible
portfolio financed.

Bpifrance will present to investors a clear distinction between the amounts issued and the
financing allocated between short-term and medium-term financing and bonds.

External Review

Bpifrance follows the commitments presented in part [2.4] of the Framework document,
thus Bpifrance has had this specific part related to short-term and medium-term financing
verified by an external third party as part of its framework document update.
