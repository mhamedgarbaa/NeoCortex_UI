1. BUSINESS ACTIVITIES

1.1. Presentation of the missions and business lines

A public group serving the financing and development of businesses, Bpifrance acts in support of the
policies conducted by the State and the Regions. The law No. 2012-1559 of 31 December 2012 tasks it
with promoting innovation, start-up, development, internationalisation, transfer and transmission of
businesses, by contributing to their financing through loans and equity.

Bpifrance is a credit institution and a holding company that acts through its subsidiaries, Bpifrance
Régions (which operates the Financing activities, together with the credit institution), Bpifrance
Participations, Bpifrance Investissement and Bpifrance Assurance Export).

As a credit institution, Bpifrance is subject to banking regulations (Order of 3 November 2014 on the
internal control of companies in the banking, payment services and investment services sector subject to
the supervision of the Prudential Supervisory and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et
de résolution)), which imposes numerous obligations on it, such as operating under market conditions,
without abusive support or ruinous credit, with a risk prevention system (financial, image and operational),
separation of roles, monitoring measurement systems and risk control. Bpifrance and its subsidiary
Bpifrance Régions are supervised by the Prudential Supervisory and Resolution Authority and subject to
direct prudential supervision by the European Central Bank1.

Bpifrance is a neutral player in the marketplace, whose role is to bring together all financing partners for
the benefit of companies, banks first and foremost, as well as innovation development networks by co-
financing with private banks.

Bpifrance provides financing to companies in their riskiest phases, such as business creation or transfer,
innovation, international development, as well as in the context of capacity investments, while respecting
the rules of competition law and private players. To do this, it relies on allocations from public actors at
different levels: State, local authorities (especially the Regions) and the European Union.

Its public interest mission requires Bpifrance to intervene as close as possible to the territories, in favour
of VSEs, SMEs and intermediate-sized enterprises, regardless of their legal status (including SSE
companies2 and local public companies3 performing a commercial activity), in all sectors of activity, except
financial services.

Its public stature also implies a special welcome for companies in each Region, a search for solutions
with all partners, more patience, an adapted remuneration.

Directly correlated to the financing granted by its partners, Bpifrance's guarantee is a crucial tool for
encouraging banks to finance SMEs in the riskiest phases of their growth, especially in the creation,
innovation or transmission phases. Bpifrance provides guarantees for bank financing (including leasing and
financial leases) and support from equity investors:

- with regard to creation;
- with regard to innovation;
- with regard to development;
- with regard to environment;
- with regard to transfers;
- with regard to strengthening the cash flow;
- with regard to international actions, the risk of failure of French subsidiaries abroad (GPI).

The standard loan-to-value ratio is between 40% and 60%. As part of a recovery plan, the guarantee ratio

The guarantee is the preferred tool for intervention, particularly with the smallest companies, as it is
implemented in partnership with private banks, which are authorised to decide immediately on granting up
to €200,000, or by requesting Bpifrance for higher amounts.

The student loan guarantee, a scheme carried out on behalf of the State and greatly strengthened under
the recovery plan, makes it possible to guarantee loans made by banks for students' benefit without the
latter having to provide other guarantees.

1.1.4.1. Tasks of the Investment Division Business Line

i.

Tasks of the Investment Division business line

As part of the creation of Bpifrance, the investment doctrine was specified during the first half of 2013 and
then adopted on 25 June 2013 by the Board of Directors of the Bpifrance Group holding company. This
doctrine, which applies to all of the Investment division carried of Bpifrance (and therefore to Bpifrance
Investissement), is summarized as follows:

Through its equity interventions, Bpifrance finances the development and growth of VSBs and SMEs, in
line with the FSI France Investissement 2020 program, and aims at the emergence, consolidation and
multiplication of mid-caps, an essential link to the competitiveness of the French economy and the
development of exports.

Bpifrance's investments, in funds as well as in companies, are carried out selectively, in accordance with
good professional practices, depending on the value creation potential (for the investor and for the national
economy) of the companies or funds financed.
However, Bpifrance is not an investor like any other. Its character as an informed investor operating under
market conditions in the service of collective interest leads him to complete the investment offer of market
segments characterised by a lack of private funds.
As a result, Bpifrance devotes a significant part of its equity investments, from its own resources and those
of third-party investors managed by Bpifrance, to the seed investment, venture capital, development capital
and transfer capital segments, or to funds geared towards profitable companies in the social and solidarity
economy but which, due in particular to their status, naturally attract few traditional investors. Bpifrance is
also working to develop a mezzanine fund offer on the market as a complement or substitute for equity
investments for companies whose shareholders would not like to open the capital to third-party investors.

In this context, all operations in which Bpifrance is involved are guided by the following principles:

 Bpifrance intervenes in order to create, via minority shareholdings, a spillover effect of private
investment through public investment. Whether it invests its own resources or those of other public
or private subscribers under its management, Bpifrance systematically seeks private co-investors
to whom it leaves the majority of shares, in order to stimulate the investment market;

 Bpifrance is an informed investor operating under market conditions. When it co-invests, Bpifrance
intervenes according to the same financial and legal provisions as the co-investors (pari passu).
Wherever possible, it sits on the boards of directors of the companies in which it has invested and
on the advisory and strategic committees of partner funds;

 Bpifrance is a patient investor. It adapts its investment horizon, particularly to the technological
context of the company, and can accept that the profitability of its investments will materialise over
a longer time horizon than most private investors. This is particularly the case in high-risk market
segments (start-up, venture capital, R&D intensive). It supports the companies in which it invests
over the long term, which does not exclude rotation of the asset portfolio in synergy with private co-
investors, with a view to good risk management, freeing up room for manoeuvre to finance new
investments and increasing the value of its assets;

 Bpifrance is at the service of the collective interest. In addition to the necessary assessment of the
financial performance of companies (profitability, sustainability, liquidity), its investment decisions
are made with regard to the impact of projects on the competitiveness of the French economy,
assessed using a multi-criteria analysis grid, integrating extra-financial criteria such as export and
international development potential, contribution to innovation, ESG (environmental, social and
corporate governance) practices, effects on employment and regional development, role in the
sector, development of family businesses, etc.



In order to ensure that its investments effectively contribute to the growth and development of

3.1. Credit risk

As a banking institution, Bpifrance is exposed to credit risk on its corporate financing transactions. This
includes:

•

default risk: the risk that a creditor will permanently lose its claim to the extent that the debtor
cannot, even by liquidating all its assets, repay all its commitments;

• migration risk: the potential loss resulting from the downgrading (or rating downgrade) of an
internal/external credit instrument or counterparty, as well as the potential indirect losses that
may result from a credit migration event;
single name concentration risk: the risk arising from exposure to counterparties considered to
be the same group of related clients
sector concentration risk: the risk arising from exposure to counterparties operating in the
same economic sector.

•

•

Bpifrance mainly deploys its financing activities for French companies of all sizes (from very small
businesses to large corporations), through a wide range of asset classes (debt financing, loans, guarantees)
and commitment horizons (short to long term). In the financing that it grants, Bpifrance ensures that risk-
taking is limited by means of (i) the implementation of co-financing involving risk-sharing with a commercial
bank, (ii) the implementation of collateral or guarantees.

Bpifrance manages its individual and global credit risk exposures based on a granting policy that defines
the characteristics of credit files and the levels of delegation according to the size of the commitment or the
sensitive and/or specific nature of the transaction. Credit decisions are based on a synthetic,
comprehensive and regularly updated analysis, the Decision Support Tool and the Fiben ratings provided
by the Bank of France. They are made on the basis of objective criteria, with a long-term perspective, a
desire for diversification, and by anticipating and taking into account changes in client ecosystems. These
decisions are monitored and reviewed on a regular basis. The Bpifrance Group is particularly vigilant about
risk concentration (by client, sector, rating) and is careful to maintain a diversified portfolio.

All Bpifrance commitments (financing, guarantee and innovation operations) involving credit risks are
subject to an initial analysis by the Financing and Network Management Department, which is responsible
for monitoring and client relations. The Credit Risk Department is responsible for the counter-analysis of
files that fall within the jurisdiction of the Credit Risk Committee and the Credit Committee of Bpifrance, and
presents them to these committees for decision.

3.2. Financial risks

3.2.1. risk of impairment of its listed and unlisted investments

As part of the investment activities of Bpifrance Participations, the Bpifrance Group is exposed to the risk
of impairment of its listed and unlisted investments.

In addition, the structure of the Bpifrance Participations subsidiary’s portfolio exposes it to the variability of

held by Bpifrance could decline, forcing the Bpifrance Group to recognise impairment charges in its
consolidated financial statements, which could have a material adverse impact on its results and financial
position.

3.2.2. Liquidity risk

The liquidity risk corresponds with the Bpifrance Group’s inability to meet its obligations at an acceptable
price, for a given location and currency. This risk can occur in case of non-concomitance of the cash flows.
The refinancing risk (an integral part of the liquidity risk) arises when the funds needed to finance the non-
liquid assets cannot be obtained within acceptable timeframes and at acceptable prices.

The Group’s liquidity risk is monitored as part of a liquidity risk management policy defined in the Group
Risk Management Policy approved by the Board of Directors. The liquidity situation of the Bpifrance Group
is assessed on the basis of internal standards, alarm indicators and regulatory ratios.

The system in place, specifically defined as part of the ILAAP (Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment
Process), provides the Bpifrance Group with the necessary liquidity to meet its short-term and medium/long-
term liquidity obligations. These measures should enable the Bpifrance Group to fulfil its regulatory
obligations.

3.2.3. Interest rate risk

The interest rate risk is the risk that Bpifrance may incur losses due to an unfavourable change in interest
rates, particularly in the event of an imbalance between the interest rates generated by its assets and those
due on its liabilities. Bpifrance generates a significant portion of its earnings in the form of net interest
margin and as such remains highly exposed to interest rate fluctuations and changes in the yield curve,
particularly in its Financing activities.

Interest rate risk management aims to control the level of change in Bpifrance’s interest rates so as to
contribute to its development while smoothing out the impact of any unfavourable movements in interest
rates over the net interest margin, both in terms of short-term impact on NBI (revenue risk) and on the
present value of future cash flows (value risk).

The institution’s interest rate risk is assessed using several indicators, calculated and presented monthly
at the ALM Committee of Bpifrance.

Interest rate risk is also reviewed quarterly by the Risk Committee and by the Board of Directors, after
presentation to the Group Risk Management Committee.

3.2.4. Exchange rate risk

Bpifrance is exposed to foreign exchange risk, which is the risk that changes in currency exchange rates
will adversely affect the value of the instruments and therefore generate losses on capital borrowed or
loaned in currencies other than the euro.

Foreign exchange risk is sub-divided into two categories:

• Structural exchange rate risk is incurred on long-term investments in foreign currencies and/or
equity consolidated in foreign currencies in shareholder’s equity. At present, this concerns the
activities of Bpifrance Participations;

• The operational currency risk is incurred by financing activities such as loans, borrowing and
placements, which generate revenue and costs in foreign currencies (NBI, costs, risk cost)
and/or by positions arising from the backing of assets and liabilities in different currencies. This
is essentially refinancing activities in foreign currencies and export financing.

Bpifrance has no direct appetite for this type of risk. It is therefore desirable that foreign currency positions
remain at a marginal level of exposure in view of the size of Bpifrance’s balance sheet.

The measurement of operational currency risk is presented monthly to the ALM Committee of Bpifrance.
The measurement of operational foreign exchange risk is presented quarterly to the Group Risk
Management Committee. If the defined alert threshold is exceeded, the Finance Department informs the
ALM Committee that the alert threshold has been exceeded and the corrective actions taken to remedy the
situation. Information is presented to the Group Risk Management Committee to report on the crossing of
the threshold and the corrective actions taken.

3.3. Operational and non-compliance risks

The Bpifrance Group’s operational risk is the risk of loss resulting from faulty or inadequate internal
processes (in particular those involving staff and IT systems) or external events, whether deliberate,
accidental or natural (floods, fires, earthquakes, terrorist attacks, etc…). Operational risk covers fraud, risks
related to human resources, legal and reputational risks, risks of non-compliance, risks related to
information systems, the provision of inappropriate outsourced services as well as risks of failure of
operational processes including credit processes, or the use of a model (model risk).

The Bpifrance Group has a governance and operational risk management system, the main purpose of
which is to ensure overall control of operational and non-compliance risks as well as to provide reasonable
assurance that the objectives set by the Group are achieved. The framework linked to this system is defined
in the Bpifrance Internal Control Charter, which constitutes the reference framework for internal control
within the Group. The Operational Risk Management Policy (PGRO) applies in strict compliance with
regulatory obligations and is aligned with the classification of operational risks established by the Basel
Committee (article 324 of regulation 575/2013 of the European Parliament). At the level of the second line
of defence, the Compliance and Permanent Control Department, and more specifically the Operational Risk
Department within it, is responsible for the organisation and functioning of the operational risk and non-
compliance control system.

3.3.1. Cybersecurity, interruption, and malfunction risk

The Bpifrance Group is faced with cyber risks, i.e., the risk caused by a deliberate act of sabotage by a
third party affecting the integrity of the Group’s information systems, with the aim of harming the company;
a cyberattack by a third party on the Group’s information systems with the aim of misappropriating Group
securities or funds. Cyber risk constitutes one of Bpifrance’s priorities in terms of operating risks. The
Bpifrance Group’s information assets are exposed to new, complex and changing threats that could have
a significant impact on the Group in terms of both finances and reputation. The professionalisation of the
criminal or state organisations behind cyberattacks has led the regulatory and supervisory authorities to
invest in risk management in this field.

The Bpifrance Group is also exposed to the risk of interruption or operational malfunction of a clearing
agent, foreign exchange markets, clearing houses, custodian banks or any other financial intermediary or
external service provider that it uses to execute or facilitate its financial instrument transactions.

In order to cope with these new challenges, Bpifrance has adopted a global approach to digital risk and has
defined a security, protection and resilience framework for the bank’s information systems in order to ensure
their availability, confidentiality, integrity and traceability. Cybersecurity risk cuts across all of the Group's
activities. The Information Systems Security (ISS) imperative is part of the Corporate Responsibility
approach. Therefore, the main ISS challenges for Bpifrance are to:

• Protect the information systems and the data entrusted to them against any form of accidental or

intentional threat;

• Contribute to the overall performance of information systems and the reputation of Bpifrance;
• Contribute to the sustainability of Bpifrance's activities and services and contribute to the overall

resilience of critical processes; and

• Allow Bpifrance to implement appropriate security measures integrating the best cost/benefit ratios.

3.3.2. Fraud risk

Like the majority of companies, all sectors combined, the Bpifrance Group is faced with the risk of fraud, which
can be committed both internally and externally. The risk of internal fraud includes unauthorized activities and
acts of internal malice, such as the theft or misappropriation of property or valuables by an employee, as well
as risks of corruption and fraud (collusion / bribes or bad in competition with suppliers, inadequacy of the
system for combating corruption and influence peddling, etc.).
In terms of external fraud, the Bpifrance Group is exposed to the risk of embezzlement, falsification or
concealment of documents or identity theft by a third party, also including the risk of cyber-attack or breach of
integrity of computer systems. In particular, the Bpifrance Group has to deal with new, more sophisticated
operating methods in terms of cyberattacks, as well as loan applications accompanied by fraudulent
documents.

Several actions have been carried out in this regard, in particular through the strengthening of the first- and
second-level permanent control mechanism, and the implementation of tools to combat the risks of document
fraud, on means of payment and fraudulent transfers. A comitology dedicated to managing anti-fraud actions
at the Group was also introduced in 2021, providing Management with visibility as regards the Group’s level
of exposure to this risk, as well as for deciding on and implementing corrective actions. As the proactivity of
these teams in the event of fraud is crucial for the Group, awareness-raising actions for employees on this
subject have been rolled-out in 2022, in particular through a compulsory e-learning course.

Non-compliance risk is the risk of legal, administrative or disciplinary sanction, significant financial loss or

customers, to file documents relating to "Know Your Customer" (KYC) and to make the data and links between the
various client companies more reliable. A supplier evaluation system has also been put in place.

Note 2 - EVENTS DURING THE PERIOD

2.1 Significant events during the period

Calculation of expected credit losses

Update of the forward-looking scenarios

Details of the scenarios
In June 2023, Bpifrance updated the four forward-looking scenarios.
These scenarios take into account several constraints on the French economy. In particular, it is
assumed that supply will continue to be affected by persistently high production costs and by
recruitment difficulties affecting all sectors of activity. Thus, inflation, although falling, would
remain high in 2023 and 2024; and the ECB continued to raise its key rates through the summer
of 2023, keeping them at high levels in 2024 and 2025. Against this backdrop, household
consumption and private investment are likely to be sluggish. Activity is therefore expected to
slow in 2023, with a limited rebound in 2024. Nevertheless, the recovery plans, in particular the
"France 2030" plan, would help sustain growth.
The scenarios also take into account the economic consequences of the ecological and energy
transition (EET), which would take the form of a negative supply shock, at least temporarily,
weighting on potential growth and supporting inflation over the entire forecast period (2023-2028).
Uncertainties about growth prospects remain high. The optimistic scenario anticipates more
dynamic growth in 2024, due to a more rapid easing of price pressures, implying more dynamic
consumption and a more rapid normalisation of monetary policy. Conversely, the downgraded
scenario (inflation and a bond crash) envisages longer-lasting inflationary pressures fuelled by a
price-wage spiral. The ECB would then adopt a more restrictive monetary policy, leading to
greater stress on sovereign interest rates. This would have a lasting impact on France's
productive fabric and on the ecological and energy transition, resulting in reduced potential growth
in the medium term. The alternative L-shaped scenario assumes a strong recessionary shock of
a health, financial or cyclical nature, followed by stagnation due to a lasting impact on French
production capacity, resulting in a fall in inflation and permanently low interest rates.

Change in weightings
The weightings of the June 2023 scenarios have been adjusted from the previous year. The
choice of these weightings is largely based on that of the Caisse des Dépôts Group.

Methodological adjustments to the model

Correlation between growth rate and default rate
The health crisis context of the years 2020 and 2021 required the application of a methodological
adjustment to the Bpifrance ECL (Expected Credit Losses) calculation model, to correct the
observed decorrelation between the economic context and the default rates.

Bpifrance does not include the data from 2020, 2021 and 2022 in the calibration of the ECL
calculation model as at 30 June 2023, and continues to use the parameters used at the end of
2019.

The effect of not recalibrating the model is an increase in ECL of €12m at 30 June 2023 (€106m
at 31 December 2022).

Correlation between inflation rate and default rate
The economic context of 2022 and 2023 translates into sharply rising inflation projections, at
levels well above the values present in the calibration history of the models. The correlation
observed historically in Bpifrance portfolios between inflation and default is negative: rising
inflation is associated with a decrease in defaults.
Therefore, updating this projected inflation has a strong counter-intuitive effect in the results of
the model, as it would result in a large decrease in the calculated amount of ECL. This result is
explained by the change in the current inflation regime compared with the last ten years and does
not correspond to the expected effects of this shock on French business failures.
To mitigate this effect, Bpifrance has decided to neutralise the effect of inflation in the update of
the ECLs, by retaining in its macroeconomic scenarios the inflation trajectories defined at 31
December 2021.
The effect of this neutralisation of inflation effects is an increase in the ECL of €161m at 30 June
2023 (€198m at 31 December 2022).

Sector-based adjustments

Inflationary shock
Given the ongoing supply difficulties and the inflationary environment (rising energy, raw
materials, and labour costs), the rating adjustments made at 31 December 2022 have been
maintained at 30 June 2023. The agri-food sector was downgraded another rank.
This adjustment results in an ECL increase of €111 million at 30 June 2023. It was €108 million
at 31 December 2022.

Photovoltaic energy
The Decree of 26 October 2021 provided for the revision of feed-in tariffs for certain photovoltaic
installations. This resulted in a very significant drop in future income from these facilities. As a
result, the ability of the companies concerned to repay their debt was uncertain. On 27 January
2023, the Conseil d'Etat annulled this decree.
This decision means that the €10m adjustment booked at 31 December 2022 must be reversed.

Other forward-looking adjustments

The cash flow of French companies has been massively supported since the spring of 2020
through the distribution of state-guaranteed loans. These loans included a one-year grace period,
which could be extended for an optional year thereafter. As a result, most SGLs have been in the
amortisation period since 2022, or will enter it in 2023. This increase in the cost of debt after two
years of deferred amortisation is causing difficulties for some companies that have benefited from
SGLs.
To take account of this risk to companies' ability to repay, an adjustment of €100 million to the
ECL has been recognised alongside the results obtained from the models at 30 June 2023. Loans
to companies identified as presenting a risk in terms of their ability to repay their SGLs are
classified in bucket 2.
This adjustment was €110 million at 31 December 2022. This decrease mainly results from the
amortisation of the portfolio in question.
