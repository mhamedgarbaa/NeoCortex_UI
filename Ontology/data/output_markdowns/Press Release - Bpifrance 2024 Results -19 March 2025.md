2024 FINANCIAL RESULTS
ROBUST PERFORMANCE: NET INCOME OF 986 MILLION EUROS

PERFORMANCE

In 2024, Bpifrance injected €60bn1 into the French economy. This high level of activity was accompanied by robust
financial performance.

In 2024, net consolidated income remained at a high level of €896m.

The Net Banking Income, for a total of €2.1bn, is comprised of:

• A net interest margin of €720m, up 9% during a year characterised by long-term interest rate instability;
• Dividends received from direct equity interests, which remain at an exceptional level of €613m (+3%);
• A contribution from the Fund of Funds business that, at €140m, remains moderate due to new caution
surrounding risk capital valuations and despite the positive signal of a very strong upturn in distributions.

The increase in expenditure reflects the growth of our activities and is in compliance with our management ratios
(operating ratio* for the banking business line and expense ratio* for the Investment business).

The rise in the cost of risk reflects a normalisation of the cost of proven risk following the exceptionally low post-
Covid years and a lower write-back of provisions for expected cost of risk.

The contribution to income of holdings in equity-consolidated companies* is down by 42% at €183m as a result of
the fall in STMicroelectronics’ net income in 2024.

In addition to net income, Bpifrance’s overall performance is evaluated over the long-term through the change in the
value of its portfolios. The change in value of Bpifrance since its creation in 2013 is €13.9bn, i.e. average annual
performance of 5.2%.

FINANCIAL ROBUSTNESS

The robustness of the financial structure is confirmed at the top level by ratios that far exceed regulatory
requirements:

• Bpifrance’s solvency remains robust: the CET1* ratio stands at 27.36% compared to a regulatory requirement

for overall solvency of 13.27% (excluding Pillar 2 Guidance);

• Bpifrance boasts an exceptional liquidity position with a liquidity coverage ratio (LCR*) of 280% (prudential

requirement of 100%), as well as a liquidity reserve of €19.7bn.

Equity-consolidated companies (ECC): equity interests over which the Group exercises joint control or significant influence are
accounted for using the equity method. This consists of replacing the value of the securities by the share that the Group holds in
the equity and income of the companies in question. Thus, the income statement reflects the Group’s share in the income of the
companies accounted for using the equity method and when an impairment is noted, this is charged to the equity-consolidated
holding on the balance sheet, which allows for the subsequent reversal of the impairment in the event of an improvement to the
going concern or market value.
